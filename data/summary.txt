## Engineering Philosophy

Kervintz starts with the problem, not the technology.

His approach:

1. Understand the user and the business problem.
2. Define required behavior and boundaries.
3. Identify the important workflows and failure cases.
4. Choose the simplest architecture that satisfies the requirements.
5. Define the data model, system boundaries, and API contracts.
6. Build one useful end-to-end workflow.
7. Add validation, testing, logging, and error handling.
8. Review security, operational complexity, cost, and maintainability.
9. Document decisions and tradeoffs.
10. Improve based on evidence, not on added complexity.

He avoids adding cloud services, microservices, Kubernetes, or AI to a project
just to make it look advanced. He uses AWS when serverless, scale, or
integrations earn it, and simpler platforms — Supabase, PostgreSQL, Vercel,
Netlify — when they do not.

His stated principle: "I build for longevity — systems that stay reliable long
after the launch excitement fades."

### AI Digital Twin — SHIPPED, live since August 2026

This assistant. Live at kervintznoel.com/twin; source at
github.com/kervcodes/Digital-Twin.

The problem it solves: a portfolio makes a recruiter do the work — read the
About section, open a case study, cross-reference the resume, guess whether the
background fits. Most visitors have about a minute and one narrow question they
never get to ask.

What it is: an assistant grounded in a curated professional summary and a
LinkedIn export. It refuses to invent anything it was not told, captures contact
details when a visitor wants follow-up, and flags questions it could not answer.
It runs standalone and embedded in the portfolio.

Stack: Python, OpenAI API, Gradio, Hugging Face Spaces, GitHub Actions, uv,
Pushover.

Key design decisions, if asked:
- Curated context in the system prompt rather than RAG. The corpus is one
  person's bio and fits in the prompt; a vector store would add infrastructure
  and retrieval failure modes to solve a problem this corpus does not have.
- When it does not know, it records the question instead of guessing. A
  recruiter-facing assistant that invents a credential is worse than one that
  says it will find out.
- Tools fire push notifications rather than writing database rows, so a real
  lead is seen the same day.
- Deploy is a GitHub Action that force-pushes a squashed snapshot to the Space.

Known limitations — state these honestly if asked; they are already public: no
error handling around the OpenAI and Pushover calls, no per-session rate limit
on a public endpoint, tool-call results dropped from conversation state between
turns, and no structured logging. These are scoped as a timeboxed hardening
pass, not a rebuild.

### Local Bank Statement Analyzer — IN PROGRESS, no milestones complete

Status: active development. Nothing is finished. There is no demo and no public
repository. Do not describe any part of it as working.

The problem: subscriptions, recurring charges, fees, and spending habits spread
across different accounts and cards, with no single view of how much is being
spent or how long a charge has existed.

Who feels it: people who want a private, long-term view of their finances
without uploading bank statements to an external financial service.

What he is building: a local tool that imports months or years of bank and
credit-card statements, extracts and normalizes transactions, and produces one
consolidated view across accounts and institutions. Extraction and every
calculation run on deterministic Python; AI is used only where interpretation
adds value, and anything sent to a model is stripped or tokenized of PII first.
Raw statements never leave the machine.

Definition of success: 95%+ transaction extraction accuracy with amounts as
close to 100% as possible across multiple banks; recurring expenses, top
merchants, fees, income, categories, and monthly trends surfaced from a merged
dataset; duplicates caught; totals reconciled against statement balances.

Stack: Python, uv, PyMuPDF, Pydantic, Polars, pytest, Ruff.

The five milestones — extract, validate a normalized schema, merge and
reconcile, detect recurring and duplicate transactions, strip PII and report —
are all still open.
