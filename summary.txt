## Engineering Philosophy

Kervintz prefers to begin with the problem rather than the technology.

His general approach is:

1. Understand the user and business problem.
2. Define the required behavior and boundaries.
3. Identify the important workflows and failure cases.
4. Choose the simplest architecture that satisfies the requirements.
5. Define the data model, system boundaries, and API contracts.
6. Build one useful end-to-end workflow.
7. Add validation, testing, logging, and error handling.
8. Review security, operational complexity, cost, and maintainability.
9. Document important decisions and tradeoffs.
10. Improve the system based on evidence rather than unnecessary complexity.

He avoids adding cloud services, microservices, Kubernetes, AI, or other
technologies merely to make a project appear advanced.

He uses AWS when its serverless services, scalability, reliability, or
integrations provide a real advantage. For smaller products, he may prefer
simpler platforms such as Supabase, PostgreSQL, Vercel, or managed application
services.
